Global Marine Heatwave Forecast Discussion - March 2026

Observed and forecasted values include the effects of long-term warming since 1990. Values with the long-term warming trend removed are in brackets.

Current marine heatwave conditions:        

In February 2026, 25% [13%] of the global ocean experienced marine heatwaves (MHWs), which ranked 30th [79th] among all months since 1991.

Currently, MHWs are scattered across the western tropical Pacific, large portions of the extratropical Pacific, the extratropical Atlantic, North Atlantic and Southern Ocean.

Marine heatwave forecasts:

Global MHW coverage is predicted to continue rising over the coming year, to ~30% [16%] by mid-2026 and ~39% [21%] by the end of 2026. Below is a regionally refined focus:

  • Tropical Pacific - Through June 2026, MHW likelihoods are expected to increase in the eastern tropical Pacific (60-80% [20-50%]; medium-high confidence), and decrease in the western tropical Pacific (50-80% [0-20%]; low-medium confidence), consistent with the development of El Niño. By October 2026, widespread MHW (70-90% [50-70%]; medium-high confidence) are expected across the eastern tropical Pacific.
  • North Pacific - High probabilities of MHWs are forecast across the central and western North Pacific (70-100% [30-60%]; low confidence) at least through June. MHW probability remains elevated near Baja California (40-70% [20-50%]; low-medium confidence) and is expected to increase by June (70-90% [30-50%]; low-medium confidence).
  • North Atlantic - Elevated chances of MHWs are forecast through June over much of the North Atlantic, particularly inside and east of the Gulf of Mexico (40-80% [10-40%]; low-medium confidence) as well as in the seas surrounding Greenland (40-90% [30-70%]; low confidence).
  • Mediterranean Sea - MHW likelihoods in the Mediterranean are projected to remain elevated through June 2026 (30-70% [10-30%]; low confidence).
  • Southern Ocean - Areas of high MHW probabilities over the next several months are scattered across all sectors of the Southern Ocean, with the highest likelihoods southeast of Africa and Australia (60-90% [40-70%]; low-medium confidence).
